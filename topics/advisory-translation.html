Offering 05 · Idea ↔ clinic

Advisory & Translation

Most health innovations don't fail on the science. They fail where they meet the work: the workflow, the team, the moment of decision. That meeting point can be found, and tested, before launch.

01

The premise

For 30 years John has done the same thing in different materials: define a clinical need precisely, then build what meets it. Sometimes that has been software, sometimes hardware, sometimes a workflow, a team, or a whole system of care.

He now brings that practice to academic investigators and commercial teams, with a focus on healthcare systems and wellness applications. The aim is clinical impact: a product or program that real clinicians and patients adopt, not one that only works in the pitch.

02

What an engagement covers

  1. The need. Whose problem is it, when does it occur, and what does solving it change?
  2. The fit. Where the product touches the workflow, and what the team has to do differently.
  3. The evidence. What buyers, clinicians, and funders will need to see, and how to generate it early.
  4. The partners. Matching the idea to the clinical, academic, and data partners who can carry it forward.
03

Signature stories

Commercial · pre-launch

Testing a device against the work before the market does

Baymatob brought Oli, an AI-guided postpartum hemorrhage monitor with FDA Breakthrough Device Designation, to Jump before its U.S. launch. John designed and led a simulation study with urban and rural labor and delivery teams. The findings gave the company pre-launch clarity on integration points, pricing, workflow, and health-economic value, and ANDHealth later featured the study as its model case for entering the U.S. market.

Lesson: the cheapest place to learn whether an innovation fits the work is before anyone depends on it.

ANDHealth with HEI Partners, Building the Bridge, 2025 · Full case on the patient safety page

Academic · at scale

An incubator for clinical–academic projects

John founded the Innovation Academic Incubator, which has launched 308 funded clinical–academic projects, awarded $24.7 million across four university partnerships, and formed 69 research teams since 2014. Each project starts from a clinical need and pairs it with the engineering, design, or data skills to address it.

Lesson: translation is a pipeline you can build, not a lucky accident.

Hardware

From a clinical gap to a patented design

John has published designs for inexpensive simulators, including models for cricothyrotomy, epistaxis, and melanoma screening, and holds a U.S. simulator patent with two further patent applications.

Lesson: a well-defined need often points to a simpler, cheaper build than anyone expected.

Ventures & ecosystem

Mentoring founders and building the region

John is Clinical Professor in the Landuyt Center for Entrepreneurship at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and a mentor in the Cozad New Venture Challenge. He co-authored the founding grant for Peoria's Distillery Labs innovation hub and serves on its board.

Lesson: founders move faster when someone who knows the clinic is in the room early.

04

Teams will

  1. State the clinical need precisely enough to design, test, and sell against.
  2. See where their product meets real workflow, and what has to change for adoption.
  3. Plan the evidence buyers and clinicians will ask for, and generate it before launch.
  4. Connect with clinical, academic, and data partners suited to the next stage.
