Offering 03 · Place ↔ behavior

Space as a Leadership Tool

Leaders design a space once and live with it for decades. Usually the behavior it produces is discovered only after everyone moves in. It doesn't have to be that way.

01

The premise

Space is a quiet form of leadership. It decides who meets whom, what is visible, what can be rehearsed, and whether it feels safe to try something new. John calls this experiential territory: the physical and designed environment in which people learn, collaborate, and make decisions.

Simulation adds something most leaders never get: the chance to test a space before anyone depends on it. You can walk a team through a new building, a new workflow, or a new process with realistic scenarios, and find the hazards and friction while they are still cheap to fix.

02

Four questions to ask of any space

  1. Who meets here? Which collisions does the space make likely, and which does it prevent?
  2. What is visible? Can people see the work, the patient, the customer, each other?
  3. What can be rehearsed? Where do people practice before it matters?
  4. Is it safe to try? Does the space invite experiment and honest reflection?
03

Signature stories

Rehearse the building

Finding hidden hazards before the first patient

John and colleagues designed an in-situ simulation to reveal latent environmental hazards in a new clinical space before it opened. Clinicians ran realistic scenarios in the actual rooms, and the problems surfaced in rehearsal instead of in care.

Lesson: a space can be tested like a prototype. Test it before the ribbon-cutting.

International Meeting on Simulation in Healthcare, 2011

Practice in place

Bringing simulation into the real workplace

John designed and taught in-situ simulation programs in operating rooms and pediatric resuscitation settings across OSF HealthCare. He and colleagues also developed a low-cost way to bring remote telehealth team members into simulations held on site.

Lesson: the best training room is often the room where the work happens.

Simulation in Healthcare 2019

Find risk early

Turning debriefs into hazard analysis

Simulation debriefs can work as a needs assessment that looks forward. Structured conversations after a scenario identify risks to quality before they cause harm.

Lesson: the people who use a space know its hazards first, if you give them a structured way to say so.

Joint Commission Journal on Quality and Patient Safety 2024

Spaces for innovation

From simulation center to innovation hub

John designed Jump, which opened in 2013, and has been its Executive Director since 2012. He co-authored the benchmarks for accrediting simulation centers, and co-authored the $10 million state grant, with a further $2 million federal investment, that created Peoria's Distillery Labs innovation hub, where he serves on the board.

Lesson: an innovation space succeeds when it is designed around the encounters it needs to produce.

04

Participants will

  1. See the space they lead as a design variable for collaboration, learning, and safety.
  2. Use simulation-based walkthroughs to find hazards and friction before a space opens or a process launches.
  3. Design learning and innovation spaces around the behaviors they want.
  4. Apply prospective hazard analysis to a planned change in their own organization.
