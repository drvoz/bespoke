Offering 04 · Those who do ↔ those who discover

Crossing the Blood–Brain Barrier

Every organization has a gap between the people who run the work and the people who could study and improve it. In healthcare it is clinicians on one side, the blood, and academics on the other, the brain. This session shows how to guide people across.

01

The premise

In business the same barrier separates operations from R&D, and companies from universities. Both sides want to work together, but they speak different languages and keep different clocks. They measure success differently, and goodwill alone rarely gets them across.

Since 2014 John has designed the workshops, frameworks, and one-to-one sessions that guide clinicians and academics across this gap. He acts as translator and navigator. The method grew from a 2008 realization: building these bridges is a skill, and it can be designed.

02

The method

The same six moves run in a room of a hundred people or across a table with two. Participants work through them on a real partnership opportunity of their own.

Explore mutual relevance

Before any project, find what each side genuinely needs and what each can offer. Exploration comes before proposal.

Match skills and data to needs

Pair operational problems with the expertise and data that could solve them, and name the gaps honestly.

Frame the hypothesis

Turn a hunch from the front line into a question that can be tested and answered.

Agree on KPIs and outcomes

Define success in terms both sides value: a publication and a performance measure, a grant and a better process.

Hold the threads

Many teams and ideas run at once. The facilitator's job is to notice which are most productive and keep them moving.

Translate and finish

Carry results back across the barrier as a publication, a pilot, or a changed practice. Provenance comes from finishing.

04

Signature stories

Inception

The conference that revealed the skill

As co-chair of a federally funded national consensus conference on simulation science in 2008, John held the threads of a national research community and carried the most productive ones into print. The result was seven papers he co-authored in a single issue of Academic Emergency Medicine.

Lesson: credibility comes from relevance, and provenance comes from finishing.

Translation

Engineers in the clinic

John co-directed and taught clinical immersion courses that place bioengineering students inside clinical settings to observe needs firsthand. He has mentored engineering teams, including one that won a national Coulter Foundation competition.

Lesson: the fastest translation is shared observation. Put both sides in the same room, looking at the same problem.

Navigation

One-to-one, as a guide

Alongside the large workshops, John works one-to-one. He helps a clinician with a question and a scientist with a method understand what the other needs, and designs those conversations as deliberately as any workshop.

Lesson: the guide doesn't own the answer. The guide makes the crossing possible.

Across borders

One idea, many institutional goals

In 2026 John received a CONFAP–Illinois Collaborative Research Grant through the University of Illinois System's Brasillinois Initiative. The project, Healthcare Systems Engineering Frameworks for Infrastructure-Driven Public Health Challenges Along the BR-319 Amazonas Corridor, pairs John with Brazilian coordinator Michele Rocha de Araujo El Kadri of FIOCRUZ-Amazônia. It is co-funded in Brazil by FAPEAM, the Amazonas state research foundation, and spans the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and Chicago campuses. The proposal was workshopped collaboratively so that one idea could serve the goals of every institution at the table: a Brazilian public-health institute, two state and national research funders, a university system's international initiative, and two Illinois campuses.

Lesson: the best partnership ideas are built to advance several institutions at once, and the method works across languages and borders.

Ecosystem

From partnership to region

The same instincts shaped Peoria's Distillery Labs innovation hub, whose founding grant John co-authored and where he serves on the board. They also inform his work as Clinical Professor in the Landuyt Center for Entrepreneurship and as a mentor in the Cozad New Venture Challenge.

Lesson: bridges between institutions are how regions innovate.

05

Participants will

  1. Map the translation gap in their own organization and identify who stands on each side.
  2. Use a structured matching process that connects needs, skills, and data to candidate solutions.
  3. Turn an operational problem into a testable hypothesis with outcomes both sides value.
  4. Leave with a design for a partnership session they can run themselves.
